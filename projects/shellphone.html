Shellphone

Shellphone was my team's final project for Northwestern's Designing Product Interactions course — a graduate studio focused on crafting rich, multisensory interactions between people and products. The brief asked us to design and build a bilateral, multisensory interaction: one you act on, and one that responds back to you across both the physical and digital worlds.

Working within the course's DPI framework, we grounded the concept in human perception, established routines, and emotional response — designing not just what the interaction does, but how it feels and what it prompts people to reflect on. We moved quickly from a wide exploration of concepts to a single focused direction, then iterated through a series of rapid prototypes and user tests.

The result was a working, hands-on demo presented at the end-of-quarter DPI expo, integrating physical materials and mechanical motion with sensors and digital feedback into one coherent, thought-provoking interaction.
